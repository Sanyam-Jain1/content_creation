# EP02: Pressure cooker ki seeti: andar kya ho raha hai?

**Hook:** Mummy: "Teen seeti aane do, phir gas band karna!" → *whistle* → "Har ghar ki awaaz. Par ye seeti hai kya?"

**Beats:** open pot stuck at 100 °C however high the flame (extra heat only makes steam) → sealed cooker: steam can't escape, pressure builds →
water now boils at about 120 °C, so dal cooks several times faster → the whistle is a **weight**: pressure lifts it, steam escapes = SEETI →
so it's a safety valve, not an alarm; if it clogs, the lid has a backup safety plug →
*twist:* in the mountains air pressure is low; in Leh water boils at only about 88 °C, so dal never softens without a cooker →
"Kitni seeti hui?" "Pata nahi mummy, physics samjha raha tha." *(whistles go off)*

**Facts:** Boiling point rises with pressure. Household cookers run at about 1 bar above atmospheric (about 15 psi), so they reach about 120 °C.
Cooking reactions speed up sharply with temperature (a common rule of thumb is about double per 10 °C), hence "kai guna jaldi".
The whistle/regulator weight sits on the vent pipe and lifts when the pressure exceeds its rating. Indian cookers also have a metallic safety plug
(fusible) or gasket-release window as a backup. Leh is at about 3,500 m altitude, where water boils at about 88 °C.
