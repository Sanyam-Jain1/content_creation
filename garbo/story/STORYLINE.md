# GARBO — The Spy Who Fooled Hitler

A 4-part dramatised reel series (9:16, ~90–100 s each) about Juan Pujol García,
the Spanish double agent who invented an entire spy network and helped save D-Day.
Events are real; dialogue is dramatised. Every reel carries an
**INSPIRED BY TRUE EVENTS** card.

Based on the original storyline doc (Claude Docs: "Garbo — The Spy Who Fooled
Hitler (Storyline)"), revised after research — see `../research/CHARACTERS.md`.

---

## Standalone rule (applies to every part)

A viewer may meet **any** part first. Each part must make sense alone.

| Beat | Length | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Cold open | ~3–5 s | The hook line on screen + one striking image. |
| 2. Who & stakes | ~6–10 s | ONE narrator line that says who Juan is and what's at stake *in this part*, over a name card. Never "previously on…". |
| 3. Name cards | on first appearance | `JUAN PUJOL · Spanish double agent`, `TOMMY HARRIS · his MI5 handler`, etc. Shown in every part, not just Part 1. |
| 4. One question, answered | body | Each part asks one dramatic question and resolves it before the end. |
| 5. Payoff + teaser | ~5 s | Resolve, then tease another part in a way that reads fine out of order ("How he faked his own death → Part 4"). |

Badge top-left on every frame: `GARBO · PART n/4`.

### The question each part answers

| Part | Question | Answer (inside the same reel) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Britain rejected him. How did he get into the war anyway? | He became a fake Nazi spy and invented Britain from a guidebook, until Britain itself was hunting him. |
| 2 | His wife threatens to expose him. How does he stop her? | MI5 fakes his arrest; she signs a promise of silence. |
| 3 | How do you make Hitler doubt D-Day *while it's happening*? | Tell him the truth too late, then lie when he trusts you most. |
| 4 | How does the war's greatest spy escape revenge? | He fakes his own death for 35 years, until a historian finds him. |

### Language

**English only, with real accents** (decision after the Part 1 review): Juan and
Araceli speak English with a heavy Spanish accent, Frederico with a German accent,
the officials in British RP. All lines below are written in English.

## Retention rules (apply to every part)

Learned from the first draft: silence and slow scene-setting kill reels.

| Rule | How it shows up |
| --- | --- |
| Spoken hook in second 1 | The narrator's first line is the most shocking fact of the part. No silent intro. |
| No dead air | Speech covers 90%+ of runtime; gaps are about 0.1 s; music never drops out. |
| Change every 2-3 s | Punch-in zoom on every line, speaker cut-ins, whoosh on every cut, slams, stamps, counters, maps. |
| Kinetic captions | Word-by-word, active word in yellow, key words in red. Many viewers watch muted. |
| Open loops | "There was just one tiny problem..." Each loop is closed within ~10 s, then a new one opens. |
| Narrator drives, characters punctuate | Dialogue is short and punchy; the narrator carries the story. |
| End on a cliffhanger + CTA | The last line teases the next part's hook, then "Follow for Part n". |

## Part 3: The lie that saved D-Day

**Cover hook:** *He told Hitler the truth about D-Day, on purpose. Too late.*

**Cold open:** 6 June 1944. Landing craft hit the beach. A radio operator in Madrid sleeps beside a silent receiver.

**Who & stakes:**
NARRATOR: "Hitler's most trusted spy in Britain is a Spaniard called Juan Pujol. The Germans call him Arabel. He secretly works for Britain, and his biggest job is about to begin."
Name cards: `JUAN PUJOL · double agent "GARBO"`, `TOMMY HARRIS · his MI5 handler`

**Scene 1. MI5, spring 1944. A map of the Channel.**
- HARRIS: "The landing is Normandy. Hitler must believe it's Calais."
- JUAN: "Then my spies will see an army near Dover. Tanks, camps, a famous general."
- HARRIS: "There is no army."
- JUAN *(smiling)*: "There are no spies either."

**Scene 2. Night of 5 June. Juan at the radio.**
- HARRIS: "Warn them. Tell them the invasion is coming."
- JUAN: "The truth? Tonight?"
- HARRIS: "Hours too late to stop it. Just in time to make you a prophet."
- *3 a.m.: he sends the call. Madrid doesn't answer. The clock spins. 8 a.m.: a reply, with troops already on the beaches.*
- JUAN *(dictating his complaint)*: "I cannot accept excuses or negligence."

**Scene 3. Madrid. Frederico reads the delayed warning.**
Name card: `"FREDERICO" · his German handler`
- FREDERICO *(slamming the desk)*: "He warned us! And nobody was listening!"

**Scene 4. 9 June. Juan's most important message.**
- JUAN *(dictating)*: "Normandy is a diversion. The main blow will fall on Calais."
- *In a German war room, a hand moves the pins back to Calais. Divisions that could have reached Normandy wait for an attack that never comes.*

**Payoff:** 29 July 1944. German radio: an Iron Cross for their star agent. Juan laughs out loud. In November, King George VI gives him an MBE.
- HARRIS: "Decorated by Hitler and by the King. In the same year."

**Teaser:** *When the war ended, the Nazis wanted to know who had betrayed them. So Garbo had to die → Part 4.*

## Part 4: The death that wasn't

**Cover hook:** *In 1949, his wife was told he had died in Africa. He hadn't.*

**Cold open:** A grave marker in the African heat. Then the same man, alive, older, behind a bookshop counter.

**Who & stakes:**
NARRATOR: "Juan Pujol was the double agent who tricked Hitler on D-Day. When the war ended, the Nazis who survived wanted revenge. So he disappeared."
Name card: `JUAN PUJOL · double agent "GARBO"`

**Scene 1. Spain, 1949. A British diplomat at Araceli's door.**
Name card: `ARACELI · his wife, back in Spain`
- DIPLOMAT: "Madam, I'm very sorry. Your husband has died of malaria. In Angola."
- *Araceli stares past him at their three children playing.*

**Scene 2. Venezuela. A dusty bookshop. Juan, older, behind the counter.**
- CUSTOMER: "You're not from here. What did you do before?"
- JUAN *(wrapping a book)*: "Nothing interesting. I raised chickens."

**Scene 3. London, the 1970s–80s. Historian Nigel West, surrounded by files.**
Name card: `NIGEL WEST · spy historian`
- WEST: "Every old MI5 hand remembers Garbo. Not one of them knows his real name."
- *Years of dead ends. Then in 1984 a retired officer gives him the name. His researcher starts calling every J. García in the Barcelona phone book.*

**Scene 4. New Orleans, 20 May 1984. A hotel lobby. An old man stands up.**
- WEST: "Señor Pujol? … Are you Garbo?"
- JUAN *(after a long pause)*: "I was."

**Payoff:** 6 June 1984, the 40th anniversary of D-Day. An old man walks alone past rows of white graves in Normandy. No one around him knows who he is.
NARRATOR: "The man who helped save D-Day spent 35 years officially dead. Before he died in 1988, he saw his Spanish children again."

**Teaser:** *How it all began, with three rejections → Part 1.*

## What changed from the original doc, and why

| Change | Why |
| --- | --- |
| "Who & stakes" narrator line + name cards in every part | Standalone rule: a cold viewer of Parts 2–4 knows who Juan is within ~10 s. |
| Part 2 set in Hendon; adds "macaroni / potatoes / fish", the gas taps, Camp 020 | Real details from the MI5 files (National Archives, reported by the Irish Times and History.com). |
| Araceli's outburst uses her recorded words ("I don't want to live another day in England!") | Real quote. |
| Part 3 adds Garbo's real complaint "I cannot accept excuses or negligence" | Real line from his D-Day message. |
| Part 3 introduces "Arabel", the Germans' codename for him | Makes "Hitler's most trusted spy" concrete for cold viewers. |
| Teasers name the part they point to | They work as navigation, not as required context. |
| Dialogue is English only, with heavy Spanish/German accents (Part 1 review) | Requested in the Part 1 review; the accents carry the authenticity. |
