# Idea research: what makes a "what if" reel work, and the next episodes

## What the research says

- **Animated "what if" science explainers are an active, growing format** in 2026 Shorts and Reels:
  catastrophic or absurd premises, told as high-stakes visual stories ([fluxnote 2026 guide](https://fluxnote.io/guides/viral-science-youtube-shorts-ideas-2026)).
  The genre's big channels are still large: The What If Show is listed at about 12.1M followers across platforms ([creatordb](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/whatifscienceshow/)).
- **The overused premises** (Earth stops spinning, gravity doubles, asteroid hits) are everywhere ([ghostshorts](https://ghostshorts.com/guides/what-if)).
  We stand out by making them **personal, second-person and everyday** ("your alarm clock", "your cat", "the shopkeeper"),
  and by landing a **real science twist** most viewers haven't heard.
- **Second-person "what if you…" skeleton/POV shorts** are a breakout format ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/what-if-you-spent-a-week-as-x)).
  Our version keeps that intimacy with a recurring cartoon "you".
- Retention devices that suit this format, and that we use: an on-screen hook question, an early reversal, a visible
  countdown or meter, a flash-forward, record-scratch resets, a silent beat, and an end-card question.

## Science behind the produced episodes

- **Time freeze**: frozen photons mean nothing new reaches your eyes (blind). Frozen gas molecules can't flow into your lungs or out of
  your way, and sound needs moving air ([Science News Today](https://www.sciencenewstoday.org/what-would-happen-if-you-could-stop-time-for-1-minute),
  [Futurism](https://futurism.com/the-physics-of-stopping-time-2)). This is speculative physics, framed as a playful thought experiment.
- **Eat sunlight**: skin covers about 1 % of daily energy needs, and you'd need about a tennis court of skin
  ([Live Science, Lindsay Turnbull](https://www.livescience.com/what-if-humans-had-green-skin-photosynthesis.html)).
  Another estimate puts it at about 290 h of peak sun per day's food, or skin about 300× larger ([Naked Scientists](https://www.thenakedscientists.com/node/190855)).
  "Two crackers" = 1 % of 2,000 kcal ≈ 20 kcal ≈ 1.6 saltines.
- **10× smaller**: you'd weigh 1/1000 (about 70 g, 17 cm tall). Surface ÷ volume rises 10×, so you lose heat much faster
  ([square-cube law / Kleiber's law](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/Kleiber%27s_law)). Total need ≈ 2,000 × (1/1000)^0.75 ≈ 11 kcal/day,
  so one 53 kcal cookie ≈ 5 days. Small animals survive huge falls (Haldane, *On Being the Right Size*, 1926).
  Shorter vocal folds give a higher pitch.

## Backlog: next 12 episodes (hook → twist → punchline)

Facts marked ⚠ need a source check before scripting.

| # | Premise (on-screen hook) | Twist | Punchline / device |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **What if gravity halved overnight?** | Air pressure is the weight of the air column, so it halves too: the whole planet suddenly lives at "Everest base camp" altitude ⚠ | Slam dunks for everyone… and everyone's out of breath. Device: altitude meter |
| 2 | **What if you were 10× taller?** (10× series) | Square-cube law: weight ×1000, bone strength ×100, so your legs snap on your first step (Galileo) | "Big dreams. Small knees." Series sibling to 00 and 03 |
| 3 | **What if you were 10× faster?** (10× series) | Air resistance grows with speed squared: a jog becomes a hurricane in your face; your body needs ~10× fuel ⚠ | You finish your day by 9 a.m.… and eat 14 breakfasts |
| 4 | **What if you could hear everyone's thoughts?** | People have roughly 6,000 distinct thoughts a day (Queen's University "thought worms", 2020) ⚠. That's 48 trillion a day on Earth | Most of them are about snacks. Device: thought counter |
| 5 | **What if mosquitoes went extinct tomorrow?** | Ecologists argue most mosquito species could vanish with surprisingly little ecological loss (Nature, 2010) ⚠ | "Nature's honest review: wouldn't miss them." Unusually happy ending (contrast) |
| 6 | **What if the speed of light were 30 km/h?** | You'd see the past: cars appear where they were seconds ago; colours shift as you move (Doppler) | Every video call is a time machine. Device: delay timer |
| 7 | **What if humans hibernated?** | Bears lose about 25–30 % of body weight and recycle urea instead of peeing for months ⚠ | January is cancelled. Device: calendar flipping |
| 8 | **What if you saw only in infrared?** | You'd see heat: people glow, printed text vanishes (ink and paper are the same temperature), glass looks like a mirror | You can't read… but you can find the warm toilet seat |
| 9 | **What if your cat were the size of a tiger?** | Cats share about 95.6 % of their DNA with tigers ⚠; a 200 kg cat still knocks things off tables | "It still wants the box." Pairs with EP03's cat |
| 10 | **What if the ocean were fresh water?** | Salt drives deep ocean currents, and these help keep places like Europe warm ⚠ | You can drink the sea… in your new ice-age coat |
| 11 | **What if Earth had two moons?** | Bigger, chaotic tides, brighter nights; eclipses all the time ⚠ | Werewolves overworked. Device: tide meter |
| 12 | **What if you never needed sleep?** | You'd get about 2,900 extra waking hours a year… but sleep is when the brain flushes waste (glymphatic system) ⚠ | Unlimited time, nothing open at 3 a.m. |

Good next picks: **#1 (gravity halved)** for its strong, surprising twist; **#2 (10× taller)**, which makes the "10×" trilogy a series; and **#9 (giant cat)**, which reuses
EP03's cat, a proven audience magnet.
